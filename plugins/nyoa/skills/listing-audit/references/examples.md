# Listing Audit Examples

Two worked examples: one with severe issues (graded F), one already strong (graded A-).

---

## Example 1 — F (24/40, multiple kill issues)

**Source MLS remarks (verbatim):**
> Stunning, must-see family home nestled in a quiet, established neighborhood! This beautifully updated 4 bedroom, 3 bath gem boasts modern finishes throughout. Perfect for the growing family. Walking distance to church and great schools. Don't miss this rare opportunity — schedule today!

**Kill issues:**
1. **Fair Housing violations** — "family home", "perfect for the growing family", "walking distance to church", and the "great schools" claim with no source. Three protected-class red flags in one paragraph. P0 — rewrite or pull.
2. **Cliché-stacked, zero specificity** — "stunning", "must-see", "nestled", "beautifully updated", "modern finishes", "rare opportunity", "don't miss". Eight clichés, zero concrete features. The reader learns 4bd/3ba and nothing else.
3. **No hook** — The first sentence could describe any house in the country. A buyer scrolling at 11pm has nothing to grab onto.

**Scorecard:**

| Dimension | Score | Notes |
|---|:-:|---|
| Hook strength | 1 | Cliché-stack opener. |
| Lifestyle clarity | 2 | Implied "family" lifestyle but no scene. |
| Specificity | 1 | Zero concrete features. |
| Photo–copy alignment | 3 | Couldn't tell from copy. |
| Buyer-targeting | 2 | Targets "family" but in a way we can't keep. |
| Compliance & risk | 1 | Three red-flag phrases. |
| Call to action | 4 | "Schedule today" is at least a CTA. |
| Differentiation | 1 | Could be any 4/3 in the country. |

**What I'd do first:**
1. Strip every Fair Housing violation before this listing goes back live.
2. Get the agent to name three concrete features of this specific house (not "modern finishes" — what brand of appliance? what flooring?).
3. Rewrite the first sentence around one of those concrete features.

## Example 2 — A- (35/40, minor polish only)

**Source MLS remarks (verbatim):**
> Quartz counters and a six-burner Wolf range anchor a kitchen built for someone who actually cooks. The 1924 craftsman bones — original white oak floors, leaded glass over the front door, a brick fireplace with the original tile surround — are everywhere. Three bedrooms up, plus a finished walk-up attic with a dormered window that's already wired and would make an obvious office or fourth bedroom. Fenced yard with a screened back porch, two-car detached garage, alley access. Open Sunday 1–3.

**Kill issues:**
1. **Buyer target slightly soft** — Reads to a 30-something move-up buyer who cooks, but doesn't fully commit (could be tightened with a specific scene).
2. **Photo–copy alignment unverified** — Need to confirm photos of the leaded glass, six-burner range, and walk-up attic actually exist in the photo set. If any are missing, add or note.

**Scorecard:**

| Dimension | Score | Notes |
|---|:-:|---|
| Hook strength | 5 | "Built for someone who actually cooks" — buyer self-IDs immediately. |
| Lifestyle clarity | 4 | Implied weekend cook + work-from-attic. Could go further. |
| Specificity | 5 | Wolf range, white oak, leaded glass, dormered window, alley access. |
| Photo–copy alignment | 4 | Pending photo verify. |
| Buyer-targeting | 4 | Implicit move-up cook/professional. |
| Compliance & risk | 5 | No flags. |
| Call to action | 4 | Open Sunday 1–3. |
| Differentiation | 4 | Reads as a specific 1924 craftsman, not a category. |

**What I'd do first:**
1. Verify the leaded-glass and Wolf-range photos are in the set.
2. Add one weekend-scene line to push lifestyle from 4 → 5.
3. Ship.
