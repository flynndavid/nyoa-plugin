# Offer Summary (Plain English for the Seller)

Translate a written offer into something the seller actually understands. Sellers don't want to read a 9-page contract; they want to know: how much, when, and what could blow up the deal.

**Required inputs:** Buyer name (or buyer's agent), offer amount, financing type, earnest money, closing date, contingencies, inclusions/exclusions, special terms.

**Channels:** Email + voicemail script (always pair these — voicemail to alert, email for the record).

---

## Email structure

### Subject
```
Offer received on {{address}} — {{amount}}, summary inside
```

### Opening (1 sentence)
We got an offer. Here's the short version.

### Headline numbers (4 lines max)
- **Offer price:** {{amount}} ({{above/below/at}} list by {{$x}} / {{percent}})
- **Earnest money:** {{amount}} ({{percent_of_offer}} of offer)
- **Closing date:** {{date}} ({{n}} days from acceptance)
- **Financing:** {{cash / conventional X% down / FHA / VA / other}}

### What's in the offer
- Inclusions: {{appliances, fixtures, etc.}}
- Exclusions: {{anything the buyer is asking the seller to leave out}}
- Repair credits or concessions: {{any}}

### Contingencies (the things that could blow up the deal)
List each contingency with a one-line plain-English explanation:

- **Inspection:** {{n}} days. Buyer can walk or renegotiate based on inspection findings.
- **Financing:** {{n}} days. Deal is contingent on the buyer's loan closing.
- **Appraisal:** {{yes/no}}. Deal is contingent on the property appraising at or above {{X}}.
- **Sale of buyer's home:** {{yes/no}}. {{If yes: this is a higher-risk contingency.}}
- **Other:** {{any unusual contingencies}}

### Strengths and weaknesses
2 bullets each. Plain English.

### Recommendation
What you (the agent) think. Counter? Accept? Reject? Why. One paragraph.

### Next step
Today's deadline (offers usually have a response window). When you'll talk again.

---

## Voicemail script (paired)

```
Hi {{seller_first}}, it's {{agent_first}} with {{brokerage}} — [brief pause] — we got an offer on {{address}} this {{morning/afternoon}}. It's {{amount}} with a {{financing_short}} loan and a {{closing_days}}-day close. There {{is/are}} {{n}} contingencies I want to walk you through before we respond. The buyer's response deadline is {{deadline}}, so we have until then to decide. Give me a call when you have ten minutes — I'll be at {{phone}}. Again, that's {{agent_first}}, {{phone}}. Thanks.
```

## Example email

```
Subject: Offer received on 123 Maple — $605K, summary inside

Hey Mike,

We got an offer this morning. Short version below; I'm going to call after lunch to walk through it.

Headline:
· Offer price: $605,000 ($20K below list, 3.2% off)
· Earnest money: $5,000 (0.8% of offer — on the low side)
· Closing date: June 15 (32 days from acceptance)
· Financing: Conventional, 20% down, pre-approved with First Horizon

What's in:
· Includes refrigerator, washer/dryer, all attached fixtures
· Buyer asking us to leave the dining-room chandelier (excluded from MLS)
· No repair credits requested up front

Contingencies:
· Inspection: 10 days. Standard. Buyer can renegotiate or walk based on findings.
· Financing: 21 days. Tied to loan approval — pre-approval letter is solid.
· Appraisal: Yes. Deal needs the house to appraise at $605K or higher.
· Sale of their home: No. (Good — they're not contingent on selling something else first.)

Strengths:
· Pre-approved buyer, conventional financing — clean stack
· No home-sale contingency
· 32-day close is normal, not urgent

Weaknesses:
· $20K under list
· Earnest is lighter than we'd want at this price ($5K vs $10–12K typical)
· Asking for the dining-room chandelier (small, but worth a counter)

My read:
This is a real, financeable buyer making a soft first move. I'd counter at $619K, ask for $10K earnest, keep the chandelier excluded. Their pre-approval is clean enough that I think they'll meet us in the middle around $612K.

Their deadline to respond is 6pm tomorrow. Calling at 1pm to walk through.

— Ann-Riley
```

## Hard rules

- No "this is a great offer" or "this is terrible" without backing it.
- Always quote the contingency days; sellers fixate on these.
- Always state the deadline.
- Always pair email with a voicemail when the seller is over 50 or has historically been a phone caller.
