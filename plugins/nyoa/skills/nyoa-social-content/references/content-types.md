# Social Content Types — Reference

Detailed specs and examples for each content type. When generating a week of content, mix these categories. No more than 2 of the same type per week.

## Market Commentary

**Purpose:** Position the agent as a market expert with data-backed opinions.

**Structure:** One data point + one opinion/insight + optional CTA.

**Rules:**
- Every number must be verifiable or framed as personal observation ("I've seen…")
- No "the market is hot/cold" without a number backing it
- Take a position — don't just report numbers, say what they mean
- Keep it conversational, not a textbook report

**Example (Instagram):**
```
12 homes closed in Germantown last month.
Average DOM: 9 days.

If you're thinking about selling in this zip code, you have leverage right now. Buyers are competing. Inventory is tight.

But here's the thing most sellers get wrong — they see the low DOM and overprice. Then they sit for 30 days wondering what happened.

Price it right. Let the market come to you.

#NashvilleRealEstate #Germantown #SellerTips
```

## Neighborhood Spotlight

**Purpose:** Establish hyper-local expertise. Show that the agent knows the area at street level.

**Structure:** Specific place or detail + why it matters + optional personal connection.

**Rules:**
- Name actual streets, parks, restaurants, coffee shops
- No demographic claims about who lives there (Fair Housing)
- First-person perspective is fine — "I walked past…", "My favorite…"
- Photos/video strongly recommended (suggest to agent)

**Example (Instagram):**
```
Shelby Park at 6am before anyone else is there.

This is why people move to East Nashville. You're five minutes from downtown but it feels like a different city.

The greenway connects all the way to Shelby Bottoms — 960 acres of trails right in your backyard.

If you're looking in this area, DM me. I know it block by block.

#EastNashville #ShelbyPark #NashvilleLiving
```

## Buyer Education

**Purpose:** Share practical buying advice that most agents don't say out loud.

**Structure:** One concrete tip + brief explanation + why it matters.

**Rules:**
- Not "get pre-approved" or "hire an agent" — everyone says that
- Share things the agent actually tells their buyers in private
- Be specific and slightly contrarian if warranted
- No Fair Housing language in examples or advice

**Example (X):**
```
The inspection report is not a wish list. Pick 3 things that matter for safety or structure. Let the rest go. That's how you get to the closing table.
```

## Seller Education

**Purpose:** Share practical selling advice from real experience.

**Structure:** One concrete tip + brief story or evidence.

**Rules:**
- Draw from actual listing experience
- Be specific — "the first photo" beats "presentation matters"
- No unsourced claims about what will increase value

**Example (Facebook):**
```
I'm going to tell you something your stager probably won't.

The dining table with 8 chairs? Remove 2 of them. Every staging photo I've tested with fewer chairs around the table performs better. The room looks bigger. The buyer can picture their own furniture.

Small thing. Big difference in first impressions.
```

## Behind the Scenes

**Purpose:** Humanize the agent. Show the real work behind the transactions.

**Structure:** Story + lesson or insight. Keep it authentic.

**Rules:**
- Anonymize everything — no client names, no identifying property details if deal is active
- Keep it short — the story should feel like a moment, not a novel
- Show the work — negotiations, late nights, creative solutions
- Avoid bragging — show the effort, not just the win

**Example (Instagram):**
```
Wrote an offer at 9pm last night. Seller countered by 6am. Had it under contract before my coffee.

This market doesn't sleep. And honestly, neither do I during offer season.

The part nobody tells you about being an agent — it's not the houses. It's the timing.
```

## Engagement / Question

**Purpose:** Invite replies and build community. Algorithm boost from comments.

**Structure:** One question that invites real answers (not yes/no).

**Rules:**
- Make it genuinely interesting — would you reply to this?
- Avoid "what's your dream home?" (too broad, too cliché)
- Local questions perform best
- No Fair Housing issues in question framing

**Example (Facebook):**
```
What's the one thing you wish you'd known before buying your first home?

I'll go first — I wish someone had told me how much closet space actually matters once you live there for more than a year.
```

## Testimonial Spotlight

**Purpose:** Turn a client review into a narrative social post.

**Structure:** Micro-story of the transaction + the quote + brief reflection.

**Rules:**
- Never just post the quote with "Thanks [name]!" — tell the story
- Agent must confirm they have permission to share
- Keep the story to 3–4 sentences max before the quote
- End with a reflection or takeaway, not a sales pitch

**Example (Instagram):**
```
When Sarah first called me, she'd been looking for 6 months. Two offers lost. She was ready to give up.

We changed the strategy. Stopped competing on price and started competing on terms — flexible close date, minimal contingencies, personal letter.

Third offer. Accepted.

"I honestly didn't think it would happen. Ann-Riley made me feel like I wasn't crazy for wanting to keep trying." — Sarah M.

That's the job. Not just finding the house. Keeping people in the fight until they get there.

#NashvilleRealtor #FirstTimeBuyer #ClientLove
```
