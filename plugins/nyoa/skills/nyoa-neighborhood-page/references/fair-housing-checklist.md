# Neighborhood Page Fair Housing Checklist

Neighborhood pages get sued more than almost any other piece of real-estate marketing. This checklist enumerates the specific phrases, framings, and claims that NYOA strips from any neighborhood page before delivery.

The Fair Housing Act prohibits "any preference, limitation, or discrimination" based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability. Courts have ruled that protected-class signaling can be implicit — a phrase doesn't have to name the protected class to violate the FHA.

## Always-strip phrases

These are bright-line violations. Strip them without asking.

- "Great for families" / "family-friendly" / "great schools for families"
- "Perfect for [demographic]" — singles, couples, young professionals, retirees, empty nesters, growing families
- "Quiet neighborhood" — courts have ruled this codes for "low-density / low-density-housing-stock / racial composition"
- "Diverse community" / "ethnically diverse" — courts have ruled describing diversity is itself a protected-class signal
- "Exclusive neighborhood" — codes for "exclusionary"
- "Up-and-coming" — codes for displacement / changing racial composition
- "Walk to church / synagogue / mosque" — religious signaling
- "Christian community" / "Jewish neighborhood" / any explicit religion or ethnic descriptor
- "Bachelor pad" / "perfect for newlyweds" / "couples retreat" — familial status / marital status signaling
- "Handicap-accessible" or "wheelchair-friendly" — use specific ADA features instead ("single-level", "step-free entry", "wide doorways")
- "Safe" / "low crime" / "low-crime area" — safety claims are not factual without a citation, and courts have viewed them as race-coded

## Always-strip framings

- "[Neighborhood] is the kind of place where [people who fit X demographic profile]"
- Describing the demographics of who lives in the neighborhood — even positively
- Describing what kind of buyer "the neighborhood attracts"
- "Plenty of room for the kids" / "great place to raise a family"
- Suggesting specific schools by quality
- Suggesting a religious institution by quality or community

## "Great schools" — the deepest minefield

Three rules:

1. **District names are factual.** "In the [Metro Nashville Public Schools] district" is OK.
2. **Quality claims need a current, public, citable source.** "Ranked 8/10 by GreatSchools.org (April 2026)" is OK. "Top schools" / "great schools" / "best district" without a source is not.
3. **Even with a source, the framing matters.** "Schools rated 8/10 by GreatSchools.org (April 2026)" is OK. "Top-rated schools make this a smart choice for families" is not — the second clause violates the family framing rule.

## Safe alternatives for common temptations

| Don't write | Do write |
|---|---|
| "Quiet neighborhood" | "Side streets with low through-traffic" (factual, describes geography) |
| "Family-friendly" | (Skip entirely. If the page describes a playground or a park, the park is the feature, not the families.) |
| "Safe streets" | (Skip entirely.) If the agent wants to address a concern, cite specific facts ("sidewalks, well-lit primary streets") without the safety claim |
| "Great schools" | "[District name] district." Stop there. |
| "Diverse neighborhood" | (Skip entirely.) Describe geography, housing stock, amenities |
| "Up-and-coming" | "New restaurants and small businesses have opened on [street] in the last 2 years" (factual, sourced if possible) |
| "Master bedroom" | "Primary bedroom" |
| "Perfect for young professionals" | (Skip entirely.) Describe the commute, the walking distance to coffee, the small lot size |

## Audit questions to run on every draft

Before delivering, scan the output and ask:

1. Does any sentence describe who lives in this neighborhood? → Strip.
2. Does any sentence imply suitability for a protected class? → Strip.
3. Does any quality claim about schools have a current source? → If not, strip or soften to factual.
4. Does any sentence make a safety claim? → Strip.
5. Is "master bedroom" anywhere? → Replace with "primary bedroom".
6. Are there any unsourced statistics? → Add a source or strip the number.
7. Could a fair-housing tester reading this page argue it preferences a protected class? → If maybe, rewrite.

If the agent's input contains any of these violations, the skill calls them out explicitly: "I flagged 'great for families' in your fact list — that's a Fair Housing risk. I'm rewriting around the parks and the lot sizes instead, which describes the same thing without the demographic framing."

## When in doubt

When in doubt, lean on geography and housing stock. The address, the boundaries, the architectural style, the lot size, the streetscape, the amenities, the market data. Those are facts. They don't violate the FHA.

Everything else is risk.
