## Hook (first sentence — this is the whole game)

{{One sentence. Concrete, specific, image-forward. No clichés. Names something a real buyer can picture: a renovated kitchen, a screened porch, a quiet street, a walk-up attic, a fenced yard backing up to a creek. Not "stunning" or "must see".}}

## MLS remarks (≤500 characters)

{{The compressed sales pitch. Lead with the hook, follow with 2–3 differentiating features, end with the buyer action ("Schedule today" / "Open Sunday 1–3"). No fluff. No fair-housing risk phrasing.}}

## Long description (300–500 words)

{{Three to four short paragraphs.

Paragraph 1 — open with a vivid scene a buyer can step into. What's the first thing they see, hear, or feel walking up to this house?

Paragraph 2 — the house itself. Specific rooms, specific finishes, specific dimensions. Concrete features over abstract adjectives. Tie features to use ("the third bedroom is large enough for a queen bed and a full desk" beats "spacious bedroom").

Paragraph 3 — the lot, the location, the immediate context (block, neighborhood, walkability). Verifiable claims only.

Paragraph 4 — the close. Who this home is right for + the action ("Open Sunday 1–3" / "schedule a private showing this week"). End on a buyer feeling, not an exclamation point.}}
